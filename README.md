The official impact site for HmBr Sports — telling the story of how a single shuttlecock humidifier saves trees, saves birdies, and makes badminton more affordable, then turns every match played into gear, travel, or scholarship funding.

## Part 1 — Why this site exists

### The problem this site solves

HmBr Sports has a **dual identity problem** in the best possible sense.

The same waterless, biodegradable shuttlecock humidifier can be pitched as:

- A **sustainability play** (saves trees, saves birds, cuts cork harvest, reduces freight CO₂)
- An **accessibility play** (cuts a $1,500–$3,000/year shuttlecock bill for serious juniors by 67–89%)

Both stories are true. Both come from the same physics — extending feather shuttlecock life by 3–9× depending on quality. But each story speaks to a different audience: environmental impact awards judges vs. badminton families and academies.

A traditional company site would pick one. **hmbrimpact.org tells both, and then shows how they connect through a rewards loop that turns every commercial sale into either reduced waste, reduced cost, tournament travel, or scholarship funding.**

### The .ORG Impact Awards context

This site was authored with three .ORG Impact Award category submissions in mind (using the official 2026 .ORG Impact Awards category names):

1. **Environment & Sustainability** — the trees/birdies/CO₂ narrative
2. **Education** — the Bold.org scholarship loop
3. **Community Strengthening** — the PERA rating system + HmBr Mela events

The 2026 awards window is **April 15 → May 27 submission**, finalists **August 11**, awards ceremony **October 6**. The portal caps written answers at **500 words per question**.

The page is deliberately structured so each section reinforces one or more of those categories. A judge skimming the page should be able to tell which section "belongs" to which category, but a casual reader should experience it as one coherent story.

### The founder context (essential framing)

From 2020 through November 2025, founder **Hari Gunupudi** personally humidified tournaments and provided free humidification service to academies, players, and parents — absorbing the operational cost out of pocket. For five years, he was, in practice, the scholarship fund.

This is why the Bold.org fund shows only $595 raised from 19 contributors: not because the impact was small, but because the founder was the impact. The site's *Honest Truth* section addresses this head-on. The entire commercialization story — and the rewards loop — is framed as "now the engine scales without one person's bank account."

This rationale is referenced throughout the page. Anyone editing the site should preserve that framing; it is the heart of the credibility argument.

## Part 2 — The narrative arc

Sections appear in this order, and the order is intentional. Each section prepares the reader for the next.

### 1. Hero — "Three returns: trees, birdies, futures"

Opens with the dual framing immediately. Three stat cards establish: shuttle life multiplier (3–9×), cost reduction range (67–89%), geographic spread (3 active continents + 2 pilots). The eyebrow tag carries the program slogan **#PlayMoreSaveMore**.

### 2. Impact So Far — animated counters

Six counters in two rows: environmental impact (green) on top, community impact (gold) below. The animated count-up triggers on scroll-into-view. These are the **actuals** — what HmBr has done — and they intentionally show modest, defensible numbers rather than hypothetical ones.

The scholarship counter shows the real Bold.org fund total ($595) with a direct link out to the verifiable source. Showing a small number with a verifiable source is more credible than showing a big number without one.

### 3. Field Evidence — the documented dataset

This section exists because *the numbers are real*. Eleven tournaments tracked between 2023–2025, point-by-point measurements, 399,228 points across 13,425 games. Two like-for-like comparison cards (year-over-year at All India Sub-Junior, same-brand across regional events) show 46–49% shuttle reduction.

A full data table follows, with an honest acknowledgment of the UC Baddy outlier (96% performance — included for transparency, not hidden).

**Why this section matters:** it's the empirical foundation that lets every subsequent projection number be taken seriously. Without it, the global projections in *Scale of What's Possible* would read as marketing math.

### 4. Beyond the Equipment Bill — the human angle

The most distinctive section, and the one most likely to stand out to judges. Other environmental impact stories stop at the supply chain. This one extends to the **operational impact**: feather durability → fewer cleanings → tighter schedules → tournaments finish on time → less venue energy waste, less night driving, less exhaustion.

The compound math uses tournament-guideline anchors (30-minute slots, ~280 matches on a packed early-round day, 2.5 minutes saved per match) to show ≥ 2 hours saved off the day's finish — a number every parent, coach, and umpire instantly recognizes.

Four cascade cards then walk through: schedule integrity (anchored on packed early-round days, **not** finals day), unseen carbon footprint of late-running venues, staff/volunteer hours, and the safety dimension of late-night drives home. The section closes with a reflection prompt that invites the reader to apply this to their own tournaments.

**Important framing note:** finals day handles itself — it is the packed early-round days that drift toward midnight. The site is careful about this distinction; it builds trust with anyone who actually runs badminton tournaments.

### 5. Scale of What's Possible — the projections

Two scenarios using a 5× midpoint of the 3–9× life-extension range:

- **Regional milestone:** 1.2 million shuttlecocks saved
- **Global adoption:** 400 million shuttlecocks/year avoided

The methodology footnote spells out every ratio used:

- 16 feathers per BWF-grade shuttle
- ~4 birds per shuttle
- Cork-oak (*Quercus suber*) bases
- CO₂ ranges spanning sea-freight-dominant to air-freight-dominant logistics

**The transparency is deliberate.** Judges trust applicants who publish their working assumptions and invite review.

### 6. The Story — dual framing

Two large cards, side by side. Green card = Planet's View ("save the trees, save the birdies"). Gold card = Player's View ("make the sport affordable"). A bridge quote underneath unifies them:

> *"Sustainability and affordability aren't two missions. They're the same physics — extending the life of one shuttlecock — told to two different audiences."*

This section is the editorial heart of the page. If a reader takes only one section, this is the one they should take.

### 7. The Loop — four-step closed system

**Buy → Save → Earn → Convert.** Each step depends on the one before it. The "Convert" step opens directly into the Rewards section that follows, which shows what reward points actually convert into.

### 8. The Rewards — three conversion paths

Three cards explain the three things reward points convert into:

1. **Gear** (merchandise, racquets, shoes, stringing)
2. **Travel** (flight + hotel credit for tournaments) ← the path most missing from typical "sustainable brand" sites
3. **Scholarships** (Bold.org direct contribution)

Tournament travel is intentionally elevated to Path 02 because for serious junior families, travel is often the largest line item — bigger than equipment.

### 9. The Engine — Match Challenge + PERA

Placed *after* the Rewards section by design: the reader has now seen what reward points convert into, and this section explains the rigor behind how those points are earned in the first place.

PERA (Performance Evaluation Rating Algorithm) is HmBr's open-spec rating system that powers the Match Challenge. The section shows:

- The core PERA update rule in a dark formula card
- A 30-page draft spec published openly for community review at [store.hmbrsports.com/pera-rating-system](https://store.hmbrsports.com/pera-rating-system)
- HmBr Mela: non-elimination tournaments designed to connect player clusters that don't normally meet (the Community Building angle)
- The principle that "outcome is binary; performance is continuous"

**Why this matters for credibility:** most rewards programs are black boxes. PERA is auditable. That's rare in sport, and it's worth a section that explains why it's rare.

### 10. The Live Fund — Bold.org

The actual scholarship fund, with a progress bar (currently $595 / $50,000), trust badges (501(c)(3), tax-deductible, zero fees), the real contributor count (19), and a real contributor quote pulled directly from the Bold.org fund page.

The footnote contains the **founder-context essay** that explains why the fund has held steady, ties the explanation to the 2020–November 2025 personal investment period, and presents commercialization as the unlock that lets the fund grow.

### 11. The Honest Truth — commerce as the impact engine

This is the section that's hardest to write and most important to publish: *"If the humidifier doesn't sell, no shuttlecocks are saved."* It admits openly that HmBr is a company, not a charity, and frames that admission as a strength — the business engine *is* the impact engine.

Includes a styled first-person founder's note quote that humanizes the argument. Without that quote, this section reads as business strategy; with it, it reads as moral logic.

### 12. Global Reach — three active + two pilots

Three full region cards (US, India, SE Asia) for active markets, plus a visually distinct *Expansion Track* sub-block with dashed gold borders and pilot badges for the two 2025 pilots (UK, Eastern Europe). The visual distinction lets a reader immediately tell which markets are at parity and which are still adapting.

### 13. CTA — Play more. Save more. Fund the future.

Two-button CTA: *Shop the Humidifier* (the commercial engine) and *Contribute on Bold.org* (the direct support path). The eyebrow tag carries the **#PlayMoreSaveMore** hashtag, closing the page identity loop.

## Part 3 — Design system rationale

### Typography

- **Bebas Neue** — display sans, all-caps, used for big numbers and hero stat values. Chosen for energy and badminton-court urgency.
- **Newsreader** — serif, used for section headlines, pull quotes, and editorial moments. We originally tried Fraunces but its italic *f* and *g* had exaggerated swooping descenders ("WONK" forms) that read as broken letterforms. Newsreader has clean modern italic shapes with the same premium editorial feel.
- **DM Sans** — primary UI/body sans. Modern, neutral, readable.
- **DM Mono** — used sparingly for technical/data moments: section labels, tournament tags, formula displays, methodology footnotes. Signals "this is the precise/auditable layer" of the content.

### Color palette

- **Green** (`--green: #1a6b3c`, `--green-deep: #0d3d22`) — the sustainability half of the brand. Cards, accents, the planet's-view duality card.
- **Gold** (`--gold: #c9a227`, `--gold-light: #f0d06a`) — the community/affordability half. Player-view duality card, Bold.org fund, pilot badges, reward highlights.
- **Ink** (`--ink: #0f1a14`) — the rigorous/data layer. Field evidence table headers, compound math callouts, the Honest Truth section background.

Green + gold are HmBr Sports' brand colors; ink anchors them when seriousness is needed.

### Verbal voice

- **Confident but not boastful.** Numbers are stated with the data behind them ("46–49% reduction on like-for-like comparisons" not "huge savings").
- **Honest about uncertainty.** The 2025 UC Baddy outlier is shown. The Bold.org fund is shown at its real $595 total. The CO₂ range is shown with both endpoints, not a fake midpoint.
- **Editorial italics for emphasis.** Italicized phrases inside prose carry the rhetorical lift: *"the tournament running late isn't a side effect — it is the cost."*
- **Reader address is direct.** "If you're a parent or a coach, you already know this part." The site assumes the reader has experience with badminton tournaments and meets them there.

### Information hierarchy

The page uses three distinct "layers" of visual weight:

1. **Editorial layer** (Newsreader headlines, prose, italics) — for narrative.
2. **Data layer** (Bebas Neue numbers, DM Mono labels, ink-on-white tables) — for evidence.
3. **Action layer** (gold/green CTA buttons, navigation) — for next steps.

These three layers alternate throughout the page. This rhythm prevents the page from feeling either data-heavy or marketing-heavy; it's a sustained alternation between *story* and *proof*.

## Part 4 — Editorial decisions to preserve

These are choices that took thought. Anyone editing the site should be aware of them before changing them.

- **3–9× life extension, never just "5×".** The capability is a range, not a point. The 5× number only appears in methodology footnotes where it's explicitly labeled "midpoint used for projections."
- **67–89% cost reduction, never just "up to 75%".** Same reasoning — the range is the honest answer. 67% corresponds to 3× life; 89% corresponds to 9× life.
- **Bold.org $595 / 19 contributors are real and verifiable.** Do not inflate. The site's credibility depends on judges being able to click through and confirm.
- **Finals day vs. packed early-round days.** Schedule-integrity claims are anchored on packed early-round days (round-of-64/32/16), **not** finals. Finals day is small (only semis + finals) and typically finishes on time regardless of humidification. The compound math headline and Schedule Integrity cascade card both reflect this distinction.
- **"Tournament travel" is a co-equal third reward path**, not a footnote to scholarships and merchandise. For badminton families, travel often exceeds equipment cost.
- **Rewards comes before the Engine/PERA section, not after.** The reader experiences "here's what the points convert into" first, then "here's the rigor behind how points are earned" second. The Loop section's *Convert* step opens directly into Rewards; Engine/PERA follows as the auditable layer underneath.
- **The founder is named.** Hari Gunupudi is named in the Field Evidence footnote, the Bold.org Live Fund footnote, and the Honest Truth founder's note. The five-year personal investment period (2020–November 2025) is the credibility backbone of the whole site.
- **UC Baddy outlier is shown, not hidden.** The 2025 UC Baddy tournament returned only 96% performance — below baseline. We include it in the data table anyway and acknowledge it in the methodology footnote. Hiding it would be more impressive but less credible.
- **UK and Eastern Europe are pilots, not active markets.** The Expansion Track sub-block visually distinguishes them with dashed borders and "Pilot · 2025" badges. They should only graduate to the main Global Reach grid when adoption is genuinely complete.
- **Path 03 (scholarship) is part of the loop, not a separate ask.** The site never says "donate to our cause." It says: buy the product, play the matches, earn the points, choose to convert them to scholarship funding if that's how you want the value to flow. This is the difference between a charity-with-a-store and a business-that-is-an-impact-engine.
- **The CSS uses real design tokens.** Colors and spacing values are defined as CSS custom properties (variables) at the top of `styles.css`. To change the brand color across the entire page, change one variable. Never hard-code values that should reference a token.

## Part 5 — Methodology references

These are the numerical assumptions used throughout the site. If any of these change, the corresponding sections should be updated together.

| Ratio | Value used | Source basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feathers per shuttlecock | 16 | BWF grade-spec |
| Birds per shuttlecock | ~4 | Industry-standard goose/duck wing yield |
| Cork base | 1 per shuttlecock | Natural cork-oak (*Quercus suber*) |
| Tournament shuttle cost | ~$20–$54/dozen | Yonex AS30 ≈ $54, TBTT Excellent ≈ $36, HmBr Blue ≈ $36, Lingmei 90Pro ≈ $20–24 |
| Annual global shuttle volume | ~500 million | Working industry estimate (not from a single source; flagged as such in the methodology footnote) |
| Life extension | 3–9× | Tournament dataset + manufacturer testing |
| Cost reduction | 67–89% | Direct math: 1−(1/3) to 1−(1/9) |
| Compound math anchors | 30 min/match, 14-hr days, 8–10 courts | Tournament guidelines |
| Conservative time saved | 2.5 min/match | From recommendation letter operational observations |

## Part 6 — Maintenance steps

This is the practical reference for the most common edits.

### Bold.org scholarship fund — five places to keep in sync

When the Bold.org fund moves, search `index.html` for `$595` and update:

1. **The counter card** in *Impact So Far*:
   ```html
   <div class="counter-card gold" data-target="595" data-format="usd">
   ```
   Change `data-target="595"` to the new amount.

2. **The Live Fund section** stats card:
   ```html
   <div class="fund-amount">$595</div>
   ```

3. **The progress bar fill width** in `styles.css`:
   ```css
   .fund-progress-fill {
     width: 1.19%; /* 595/50000 */
   }
   ```
   New width = `(new_amount / 50000) × 100`. Update the comment too.

4. **The contributor count and "% funded" line** in the Live Fund section:
   ```html
   <div class="fund-progress-meta">
     <span>1.2% funded</span>
     <span>19 contributors</span>
   </div>
   ```

5. **The Path 03 rewards card** also mentions the live fund:
   ```html
   <p class="path-example">Live fund: $595 raised toward $50,000 goal · 19 contributors · see below</p>
   ```

### Impact counters

The animated counters at the top of the page are driven by `data-target` attributes:

```html
<div class="counter-card" data-target="12500" data-format="number">
```

Six counters total: shuttlecocks saved, birds spared, trees/CO₂ saved, players reached, equipment/travel funded, scholarship fund raised.

`data-format` accepts `"number"` (12,500) or `"usd"` ($12,500).

### Tournament dataset (Field Evidence)

The full data table is hand-written HTML. When a new tournament is available, copy an existing `<tr>` row in the `<tbody>` of `table.data-table` and edit the values. Mark humidified rows with `class="is-hum"` and use the `pill pill-hum` or `pill pill-non` classes for the status pill.

Also update the stat strip above the table (tournaments tracked, games played, points counted) and the like-for-like comparison cards if the new data changes those averages.

### UK and Eastern Europe pilots

When pilots graduate to fully active markets, move them from the *Expansion Track* sub-block into the main `.global-grid` as full region cards (and remove the pilot badges).

## Part 8 — Deployment

This is a static site. Any static host works. The site currently runs on Vercel; other options are listed for reference.

### Option A — Vercel (recommended — currently in use)

1. Push this folder to a GitHub repo (already done: [github.com/gharikishore/hmbrimpact-site](https://github.com/gharikishore/hmbrimpact-site)).
2. Go to <https://vercel.com/new> → **Import Git Repository** → pick `hmbrimpact-site`.
3. Leave all build settings blank — Vercel auto-detects "Other" / static.
4. Click **Deploy**. You get a `*.vercel.app` URL in ~15 seconds.
5. In the project's **Settings → Domains**, add `hmbrimpact.org` and `www.hmbrimpact.org`.
6. Update DNS at the registrar (Squarespace Domains, for this site):
   - `A` record on `@` → `76.76.21.21`
   - `CNAME` on `www` → `cname.vercel-dns.com.`
   - Leave existing `MX` and `google-site-verification` TXT records intact — they're for Google Workspace email and are unrelated to the website.
7. HTTPS certificates issue automatically within a minute of DNS propagating.

Alternative quick path: `npx vercel` from this folder, then `npx vercel --prod` to promote.

### Option B — Cloudflare Pages

1. Push this folder to a GitHub repo.
2. Go to <https://pages.cloudflare.com> → "Create a project" → connect the repo.
3. Build settings: leave build command empty, output directory = `/`.
4. Add custom domain `hmbrimpact.org` in the project settings.
5. Update DNS to point at Cloudflare.

Pros: Free, fast global CDN, free HTTPS, no build step needed.

### Option C — Netlify (fastest first-time deploy)

1. Go to <https://app.netlify.com/drop>.
2. Drag this entire folder onto the page.
3. You'll get a `*.netlify.app` URL in seconds.
4. Add custom domain `hmbrimpact.org` in site settings.
5. Update DNS.

The `netlify.toml` file in this repo handles the build config automatically.

### Option D — GitHub Pages

1. Push to a GitHub repo.
2. In repo settings → Pages → source = `main` branch, folder = `/`.
3. Site goes live at `https://<username>.github.io/<reponame>`.
